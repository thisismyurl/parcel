This week’s single-origin: a washed Yirgacheffe from the Gedeb co-op

Dana Okafor · September 18, 2026

We picked up forty pounds of a washed Yirgacheffe from the Gedeb co-op this week, and it’s the kind of lot that makes the whole roastery smell like citrus zest for two days straight. Bright, floral, a little bit of bergamot up front, and a clean finish that doesn’t linger past its welcome.

We roasted it light, lighter than we usually run our house blend, to keep as much of that top-note brightness as we could. If you’ve only ever had it as a French press, try it as a pour-over instead. The paper filter pulls out just enough body to let the citrus lead without getting muddy.

Every bean is cupped before it’s bought, every loaf is shaped by hand, and every regular gets remembered. That’s the whole plan.

It’ll be on the board as our single-origin pour-over through the end of the month, or until the forty pounds run out, whichever comes first. Ask at the counter if you want a bag to take home; we’ll grind it fresh while you wait.

Where the beans came from

The Gedeb co-op sits at just under two thousand metres in the Gedeo Zone of southern Ethiopia, one of the higher-elevation growing regions we buy from. Higher elevation generally means a slower-ripening cherry and a denser bean, which is a lot of the reason this lot tastes the way it does.
